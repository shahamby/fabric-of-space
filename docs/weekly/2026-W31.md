# fabric-of-space — The Week of Knobs, and the Labels That Keep Them Honest

*Recap and roadmap for 2026-07-27 → 08-02, written 2026-08-01. Two editions of the same story: read the first with your feet up, read the second with a pencil.*

## Part I — The Plain-English Edition

### Where the project stood on Monday

A galaxy in a browser, weighed. Last week the model went on trial against 773 real stars and came back with a number: the dark halo makes it 148 times more right. But that verdict was a scoreboard you could only read, not a machine you could operate. Every constant in the simulation sat exactly where the universe put it, and there was no way to ask *what if it didn't*.

This week the project grew hands.

### What happened

Eleven milestones in six days, and they tell one story with one recurring twist: **every knob you add invents a new way to lie, and most of the week was spent building the labels that stop it.**

It opened with **the halo becoming a dial.** Before touching it, a day went into making it safe to touch — freezing the calibration frame so that turning the knob could never quietly rewrite the measured velocities the whole verdict rests on. That guard was tested by breaking it first: 18.5 and 36.6 kilometres per second of contamination, seen leaking, then sealed to zero.

Then the valley was found. Sweep the dark halo's mass across thirty-fold and the model's badness-score falls into a trough and climbs back out. The bottom of that trough sits at 0.993 times the house value — the Milky Way's halo mass, measured in a browser, from real Cepheid stars. It got drawn as a 44-pixel strip under the verdict panel, so you can see the whole travel at once instead of remembering the last number you saw.

Midweek came **the hunt** — four questions asked of 145 real globular clusters, cycled with one key. Three of them are pure geometry and must not flinch when the halo knob moves. The fourth divides by escape speed and must move. That asymmetry isn't a quirk; it's the proof the wiring is right.

Then **WHAT IF** opened, and it is the most carefully built thing in the project. A sandbox where constants may be moved — but never faked, and never in a second copy of the engine. One engine, two modes, no fork. Anything you've dialled is named in a banner across the top and the whole fabric turns amber, because a screenshot can be cropped and a number with no label is a lie waiting to be quoted. Entering takes a deliberate double keypress. Leaving resets everything, so there is no half-dialled world to wander back into.

With that safety net, **the black hole got a size.** Sagittarius A*'s event horizon is a real length — 2GM/c², computed, never drawn to taste. At its true mass that length is four ten-billionths of a kiloparsec, five billion times smaller than anything has ever come to it: unresolvable, so the picture still uses a stand-in and says so. Turn the mass knob up past 121 million times and the horizon finally grows larger than the grid can hide, and from that point on it is drawn at its true size. And the sheet learned to measure itself against its own far edge instead of an absolute zero, because a gravitational potential has no absolute zero — only differences are observable. The funnel got four and a half times deeper to look at, and the baseline picture moved, and that was said out loud.

Saturday the clock broke, and fixing it took the rest of the day.

### The week's honest hour

The failure worth reading is not a crash. It is a label that was **accurate and still deceived.**

When the black hole became a knob, one reader of the gravity field was forgotten: the routine that seeds the clusters' measured velocities. Turning the knob moved the Sun's ride from 232 kilometres per second to 150,180, and declared 126 real star clusters unbound — clusters that had not moved a millimetre. All the while the audit line truthfully reported "halo 1.00x," because the halo knob was indeed untouched. **A label can name the knob that did not move, and by doing so, lie about the one that did.** Eight readouts across the app now name every dialled constant, not just the relevant-looking one.

Then Saturday. The galaxy clock ticks in fixed steps of two hundred thousand years. That was fine while the black hole weighed what it actually weighs. Dial it up a billionfold and a cluster completes **nine and a half orbits inside a single tick** — the simulation there is not approximate, it is arithmetic about nothing. Ten such ticks took a body on a perfect circle from six hundred parsecs out to ten thousand kiloparsecs. Gone.

The fix chops each tick finer until the fastest thing on screen gets forty slices per orbit. All three populations of moving objects vote on how fine — and that mattered within hours, because a synthetic tracer star seeded past four kiloparsecs was found holding the vote from 0.18, having fallen inward on its own.

But a plunge is not an orbit. A body falling nearly straight in crosses the whole well in a fraction of an orbit's time, so a criterion built on orbits is reading a clock the body isn't on. Two fixes were designed for it. **Both were measured, and both were thrown away** — one turned out to be an existing setting wearing a second name, and a worse version of it; the other's results flipped sign depending on configuration, which is noise, not improvement.

What shipped instead was the truth. A body falling toward a black hole dialled ten billion times too heavy reaches **nine times the speed of light before any arithmetic error at all.** The engine is Newtonian. No amount of extra computation makes that answer mean anything. So the screen now says so, by name: *BEYOND NEWTON — this path describes nothing.* The knob stays reachable. The failure is instructive, and this project has never hidden one.

### The equations, gently

**A valley has a bottom, and the bottom is a measurement.** Make the halo too light and the model's stars orbit too slowly; too heavy and they orbit too fast. Wrong in both directions means right somewhere between, and the *place* where wrongness is least is an answer. What the width of that valley measures is how sharply it curves — not how well we know the Milky Way. The floor sits at nine, not at one, so the model's own flaws dwarf the band. That distinction is kept everywhere it appears.

**Why a horizon is a length.** Double a black hole's mass and its event horizon doubles too. It is not a fuzzy region or a drawing convention; it is a radius you can compute from the mass and the speed of light and nothing else. Which is why turning the mass knob makes it *grow on screen* — and why, for the real Sgr A*, it is honestly too small to draw and the picture admits to a stand-in.

**Why a timestep can be a lie.** A simulation moves everything forward in jumps. If the jump is shorter than the motion it's describing, the answer is close. If the jump is *longer than an entire orbit*, the answer is unrelated to the question. The cure is to make the jump smaller — until the cost of doing so exceeds any budget, at which point the only honest move is to stop claiming.

### What comes next, in plain words

The next real prize is **capture**: watching something cross the horizon and not come back. That needs a test which asks whether a *path* crossed a sphere, not whether a *point* was inside one — because at these speeds a body is outside before the step and outside after, and a naive check never sees the crossing at all. The clock now subdivides finely enough to make that test meaningful, which is exactly what this week bought.

### The three that were physics, not plumbing

**B1, the bracket bluff designed out.** HL3 is taxonomy #13's second appearance, and this time it was engineered against rather than discovered. `findFloor` is handed a window that *excludes* the floor and is required to report `edge:true`. Without that guard it would have named 1.50x house — a 51% error delivered as a measurement, with a straight face. A finder that can return its own boundary must be structurally incapable of calling that boundary a minimum.

**W2a.1, the label that was accurate and deceived anyway.** Adding MBH to `galaxyPhi` meant walking every reader of `galaxyPhi`, and one was missed: `seedClusterVelocities` at physics.js:407. The seed frame moved the Sun's ride from 232.1 to **150,180.2 km/s** and unbound 126 clusters that never moved, while the AUDIT truthfully said "halo 1.00x." The architectural rule now stated in the handoff — SEED pins to calibration, DYNAMICS honours the live knob, READOUT names what is dialled — exists because of this hour. Closed at 8 sites via `dialledInto()` / `dialledShort()`. Taxonomy #15.

**W2c.2, the two rejections.** The crux test, cap lifted to 5000 so neither candidate is throttled:

```
Sgr A* 1e9x
  target  40, travel OFF          |dE/E0| 3.44e-2  bound true   peak n 2
  target  40, travel eps 0.05     |dE/E0| 5.27e-3  bound true   peak n 5
  target 126, travel OFF          |dE/E0| 6.13e-3  bound true   peak n 4
Sgr A* 1e10x
  target  40, travel OFF          |dE/E0| 1.40e+4  bound false  peak n 908
  target  40, travel eps 0.05     |dE/E0| 1.11e+2  bound false  peak n 312
  target 126, travel OFF          |dE/E0| 6.58e+1  bound false  peak n 73

Raising `target` alone matches the proposed travel criterion at 1e9x and **beats it at 1e10x, 6.58e1 to 1.11e2, at a quarter the substeps.** Derived principled as ε = 2π/target it changes nothing at all — a feature that never moves an observable, taxonomy #7 wearing a feature's clothes instead of a test's. The adaptive per-substep loop fared no better: identical at 1e9x, and its sign flipped with configuration at 1e10x, which is chaos in an already-unbound regime.

And the convergence table, which is why chasing it is futile:

```

cap   3,000 → min r 2.29e1 kpc, E/E0 -5.33e+2

It converges. It is unaffordable. And free-fall to the 0.05 kpc clamp at 1e10x reaches **2.720e6 km/s = 9.1c before any discretisation error**, so the trajectory was never a description of anything. Ship the label, not the compute.

### The four failures, all self-caught

**Running a test changed the data (B0).** Four labs re-fetch catalogues the app also ships compiled into its bundle, and each wrote the fresh copy straight over the shipped file. VizieR stamps the fetch date into every response — so merely *running a test* changed four of the five sha256 values in CHEATS #18 without changing a single measurement. Fail-silent, with `git status` the only witness, and the casualty was the provenance panel: the one instrument whose entire job is proving the data is real. Found by B0's own workflow, because G7 requires running gaiaLab and running gaiaLab broke SN1. Re-archiving is now opt-in behind `SNAPSHOT=1`.

**The confession filed to a side channel (B3).** B3's CHEATS entry was written into the commit message (`5c52ee0`) and never into CHEATS.md. The ledger ran 22, 23, 25, 26 for three days — a numbering gap with no explanation, in the one document whose whole job is confessing everything. The confession existed. It was filed where no auditor reading the ledger would ever see it.

**The taxonomy had three numbering series (T0).** HANDOFF line 209 held a canonical block 1–7 that was never updated after M9; everything numbered since was assigned ad hoc in session prose against a list nobody re-read. Collisions at 2, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12, with the old series colliding with itself twice. Resolved into `docs/TAXONOMY.md` — items 1–7 verbatim, 8–16 renumbered from what had been earned, a citation map translating every historical mention, and one entry retired because legendLab made it structurally impossible to commit.

**The fixture that did not match the field (W2c.2).** The substep readout printed `(unnamed)` for all 126 Harris clusters, because they carry `.id` and the code read `.name`. It slipped ST6 because the lab's own fake cluster was seeded as `{ name: 'DIVER' }` — **the lab and the code agreed about a shape the data does not have.** Caught only in the browser, by reading a label where a catalogue name belonged. Taxonomy #17, new this week.

### The knob has a second memory

CHEATS #23 documented `MS` only. It applies to `MBH` too, and far more violently. Dialling the hole *up* is harmless — a deeper well binds tighter. Dialling it back *down* is not: a cluster that fell toward 4.3e16 M☉ and gained 1e5 km/s keeps that speed when the hole reverts to 4.3e6. The well vanishes; the kinetic energy does not, and the halo empties. Observed by walking `s` through 1x → 1e10x and back twice with 126 clusters in flight — innermost body left at 508 kpc, one reading 357.9c. Not a defect. The honest consequence of moving a constant mid-flight, and `u` restores byte-exact from `c.seed`. CHEATS #23 amended.

### A pre-existing bug logged and not fixed

`stepGalaxyStars` runs **199 steps when 200 are requested**. Repeated `carry -= DT` drifts microscopically below `DT` and one step is silently dropped. Unchanged by W2c and orthogonal to it. ST3 now reads the count back from `GAL_STARS.myr` rather than assuming it, so the receipt cannot be quietly wrong about how far it integrated. Fixing the accumulator would move TRUTH-mode timing, so it waits for its own milestone.

### Instrument discipline, and one vindication

The substep count was very nearly built to poll clusters only. It polls tracers and HYG stars too, on the argument that excluding a population integrates it at a resolution already declared insufficient and says nothing about it — a fail-silent channel inside the loop built to close one. Vindicated within hours in the browser: **tracer T54, seeded past 4 kpc, held the vote from 0.18 kpc** while all 126 clusters were loaded and flying. The clusters-only design would have missed it in silence.

Second: `legendLab`, built in session 14, caught three undocumented keys on its **first run against real work** in session 18. First time the guard has fired outside a drill.

### Receipts and determinism

`stepLab` ST0–ST9, FAIL-before captured against unpatched `physics.js` — ST0 FAIL, ST1 showing a circular body driven from 0.6 to **10,751.467 kpc** in ten unsubdivided steps. Cross-machine determinism demonstrated twice more this week: the full ST1–ST7 set, then ST8/ST9, reproduced digit-for-digit on Windows against values computed independently. Full gate green: lint, captureLab, legendLab, sandboxLab, starsLab, gaiaLab (G6/G7/G8 all 0.000), build.

### The roadmap, with the physics waiting on it

- **CAPTURE** — the next real prize, and the one W2c was built to unblock. A probe with its own dt, a **segment-crossing** test rather than point-in-sphere (taxonomy #16, still unreceipted and waiting for exactly this), a reaper, and a `captureLab` negative that watches a point test tunnel through the horizon and survive. Sgr A* mass must NOT increase on capture — Harris carries cluster masses but we never loaded them, and inventing one would be a physics cheat.
- **B4 shelved** — the valley strip samples at 3.606% per point, ~72x coarser than haloLab HL2's 0.05% walk, so it cannot print the sealed 0.993x / 8.96 / ±3.2%. Read the strip for SHAPE, haloLab for NUMBER. CHEATS #24.
- **B5 shelved** — `lab/ledgerLab.mjs`. eslint guards code; legendLab guards docs against code; **nothing guards the ledger.** It would have caught the missing #24 the day it happened, and now also the taxonomy citations. ~25 lines.
- **S0 debt stands** — the keydown firewall has no input guard. The moment a text field exists, typing "Terzan" fires t/e/r/z/a/n and spawns a rogue body.
