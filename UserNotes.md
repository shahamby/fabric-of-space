But there's no Access-Control-Allow-Origin header in the response. That's the whole verdict: browser fetch will be CORS-blocked. The Vite proxy is the path, exactly as the handoff predicted.
Why curl works but your browser won't — the security framing (you'll get this faster than most programmers do): CORS isn't the server refusing anything. NASA's server happily answered — 200, full payload, delivered over the wire. CORS is the browser acting as a DLP appliance on the user's behalf. Before handing a cross-origin response to the page's JavaScript, the browser checks whether the destination published an explicit allowlist header saying "pages from other origins may read this." No header = the browser receives the bytes and then shreds them at the desk. curl has no such policy engine — same request, no guard. It's egress filtering enforced at the endpoint, not the network.
